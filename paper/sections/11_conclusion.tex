\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We began with a folkloric question, how far are we from translating what a
dog says, and end with a more useful answer. ``Translation'' is not one
capability but five, and the distance to each is different in kind. Signal
detection and affect classification (Rungs~1--2) are here and shipping;
validated affect-and-intent inference (Rung~3) is a research program with a
foothold in the soundboard literature and a plausible horizon around 2030;
open referential translation and free dialogue (Rungs~4--5) presuppose a
generative symbolic system the biological evidence does not support, and
may be permanently ill-posed. The canine Rosetta Stone is a metaphor with a
catch: the original worked because one decree existed in three scripts, and
for the dog there may be no decree to transcribe, only a rich, graded,
affective life we can learn to read but not to render as sentences.

Anyone who has lived with a dog knows the scene that motivates this
literature: the pause at the door, the whine at the empty bowl, the look
that seems to wait for an answer. The information in that moment is real,
affect, arousal, intent, addressed to us. What the evidence does not support
is the next step of the fantasy, a sentence inside the whine waiting for a
decoder. Honoring the moment means reading it well, with calibrated
uncertainty, and letting the dog's subsequent behavior correct us. That is
a research program, and it is also a form of respect.

Three findings anchor the verdict. The biology: dogs signal affect,
arousal, identity, motivation, and, with human scaffolding, referential
intent, but they do not possess an open-ended generative
grammar~\cite{pongracz2005barks,farago2017growls,waller2013dogfacs,bastos2024scirep}.
The technology: the pipeline's front (encoding, structure discovery) is
maturing fast on the cross-species foundation-model boom, while its back
(grounding, validation) is where canine-specific work is needed and
commercial effort is
absent~\cite{hagiwara2023aves,robinson2024naturelm,sharma2024whale,google2025dolphingemma}.
The evaluation: headline accuracies are unfalsifiable as translation
metrics because no ground-truth intended message exists to score against;
they are, at best, Rung-2 emotion-classification
numbers~\cite{icassp2018dogbark}. The market demonstrates Rung 2 and sells
Rung 5, and the gap between those is the subject of this survey.

The way forward is better infrastructure and protocols: a public grounded
canine corpus, calibrated multimodal affect models, closed-loop blind
validation, and an ethics framework that keeps welfare as the terminal
criterion. The achievable goal is interpretation with calibrated
uncertainty, and it is within reach this decade. The dog at the door does
not need a transcript. It needs a listener. A field that builds the
instruments for that listening will have made the oldest conversation
between our species a little more honest, and a little more kind.

\subsection{Limitations of this survey}
This is a narrative survey, not a PRISMA systematic review; sources were
gathered through structured search in November 2026, and coverage is broad
rather than exhaustive. Several fast-moving items (WhAM, ZF-AIM, vendor
metrics) rest on project reports and journalism rather than peer review and
are labeled as such; commercial accuracies are reported as claims and were
not independently verified. Some citations were confirmed to venue, year,
and DOI via secondary indexes and should be checked against the primary
record before reuse. The distance estimates are expert judgments, not
forecasts, and Section~\ref{sec:future} states what would falsify them.
These limitations bound the central claim, which is the point.
